— How to Play —

The rules. That's the whole game.

1

Customers arrive with an order. The ticket lists exactly what they want.

2

Tap ingredients to build the order on your plate. The order you tap them in doesn't matter.

3

Ingredients are split across three counters — Sweets, Savory, and Drinks. Switching between them never clears your plate.

4

Tap the customer to serve them. Everything on the ticket, and nothing else — an extra pickle is a wrong order.

5

Serve fast for more money, and keep a streak going for more still.

6

Nobody waits forever. Enough customers walking off ends the day early.

7

Earn stars to unlock new fairs, and spend your takings on new things to sell.

8

Need a moment? Pause any time — the day waits right where it is.

— The Rush —

A 120-second day that gets meaner as it goes.

Nothing below is rounded off for effect — this is what the code actually does.

⏱

Patience Runs Out Faster Late

Every customer's patience drops as the day runs, down to a hard floor near closing — so the last stretch of a day is nothing like the opening bell. How forgiving that clock is depends on which stand you're working.

💰

Speed and Streak Both Pay

Serve the moment a ticket lands and earn up to +50% on top of price. Chain correct orders and each one pays another +25%, stacking up to an 8-streak cap.

🚫

Too Many Walk-Offs Closes the Stand

A wrong plate costs the streak and 2 seconds off the clock — but the customer stays. Let enough walk off from waiting too long, though, and the day ends early instead of running the full clock.

🔔

Rush Hour

On some rounds from the County Fair on, a bell clangs partway through the day and the crowd surges: customers come in twice as fast, and every order pays +50% for 20 seconds. Not at the Hot Dog Stand — learn the ropes first.

— 1.3: Redrawn —

Every bite redrawn. The crowd talks back.

All 48 ingredients and 14 dishes now match, as one set. 36 customers speak — a thank-you, a complaint, a goodbye — with a voices switch on the map if you'd rather it stay quiet.

— On the Menu —

Fourteen dishes. Every one has a catch.

Required ingredients make the dish. Optional ones — the ones a customer might or might not want — are the entire reason two hamburger tickets are never the same order.
